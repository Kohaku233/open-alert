With a price alert, you set the target price yourself. Anomaly monitoring needs no price level. The system scans a large number of contracts on its own and tells you when a symbol's price move and trading volume are both far above their usual levels.

## How it differs from price alerts

| | Price alerts | Anomaly monitoring |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who sets the condition | You set a target price | The system decides using statistical rules |
| Coverage | Markets you choose | USDT perpetual contracts on Binance, OKX and MEXC |
| Switch | Monitoring switch | Anomaly pushes switch. The two are independent |
| Frequency | Each alert triggers once | The rules are strict, so expect a few per day on average |

## Which contracts are monitored

- USDT perpetual contracts on Binance, OKX and MEXC.
- 24-hour trading volume of at least $10 million.
- Crypto contracts must also match a token on CoinGecko with a circulating market cap of at least $10 million.
- Stocks & TradFi contracts (such as contracts tied to US stocks) form a separate category that you can turn on or off on its own.

## How to turn it on

1. On the **Notifications** page, under delivery rules, link "Crypto anomalies" or "Stock anomalies" to at least one enabled notification channel.
2. Go to the **Anomalies** page, turn on the **Anomaly pushes** switch, then turn on **Crypto contracts** and **Stock & TradFi contracts** as needed.

If no channel receives anomaly messages yet, the page shows "No enabled channel receives anomalies, so nothing can be pushed." and you can't turn on the switch.

<Aside type="caution" title="Anomaly alerts are not trading signals">
Anomaly monitoring catches unusual market moves that have already happened. Its job is to prompt you to check the market in time. It can't predict where the price goes next.
</Aside>
